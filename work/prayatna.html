The design problem

Cafe Dil, short for Dignity in Life, is a cafe run by Swayam, a program under Prayatna.

It had to work like any other cafe. People walk in wanting coffee, and nothing about the experience should ask them to adjust their expectations.

It also had to work as a workplace for staff who have learned through pictures since they were young. These were two design problems sharing one identity, and almost every decision came down to which audience a given piece was for.

What I owned

I designed the identity, menus, signage, cafe banner, staff picture aids, and launch film. I worked directly with Prayatna's founder-trustees throughout, designing within the colors and visual direction they had in mind for the cafe.

My role was to make one identity work across the customer experience, the staff's daily routines, and the launch.

The identity

I incorporated artwork by Prayatna's students into the logo, giving the identity a personal connection to the people at the heart of the cafe.

The challenge was making the artwork work as a practical logo: at small sizes, in a single color, and on a banner that could be read from across the street.

For customers

The customer-facing menus, signage, and banner needed to make the cafe look and feel welcoming, while keeping it easy to recognize and navigate.

For the staff

The staff at Prayatna have used picture aids as a learning tool since they were young. The idea of a picture-aid menu came from the founder-trustees, Mridula and Radiya: rather than introduce a new system, the staff's menu would build on the format they already knew. My job was to design it.

I created a picture-based version of the menu using photographs of each item, with color coding to distinguish vegetarian and non-vegetarian food, as well as hot and cold drinks. The aim was to create a menu in line with how the staff already learn and communicate.

The launch film

The identity also needed to extend beyond the cafe itself. I directed, shot, and edited the launch film to introduce Cafe Dil and the people behind it.

My other films for Prayatna are in the gallery.

Where it landed

Cafe Dil is open and running, with around 30 visitors a day. The picture menus are part of the daily routine for its 20 or so baristas, built around a format they were already familiar with.
